Self-study pilot / browser simulation

Move a two-link robot arm

Predict a robot's position. Test the geometry. Find two ways to reach the same point.

No installation. No physical robot. An idealized, two-dimensional model.

Try the modelDownload model codeDownload offline lessonDownload worksheet

Before you start

You need a modern browser, basic trigonometry and a way to record your calculations. All inputs are supplied on this page. JavaScript runs locally in your browser; no account is required and no results are uploaded.

Learning goals: distinguish joint angles from end-effector position, calculate forward kinematics, recognize multiple joint solutions and explain reach limits.

1. Understand the model

Two rigid links each have length 1 m. The base is at (0,0). Joint 1 is measured counterclockwise from the positive x-axis. Joint 2 is measured relative to the first link. Both joints are ideal and can rotate from -180 to 180 degrees.

With angles converted to radians, the end point is:

x = L1 cos(q1) + L2 cos(q1 + q2)
y = L1 sin(q1) + L2 sin(q1 + q2)

Each term is the horizontal or vertical projection of a link. The second link's world angle is q1 + q2. This is geometry only: there is no dynamics, gravity, collision checking, torque limit, sensor noise or hardware control. Do not transfer these joint commands to a real robot.

2. Predict before you move

Calculate the end point for q1 = 0°, q2 = 0°. Repeat for (90°,0°), (0°,90°) and (45°,-90°). Write your predictions before revealing the reference answers.

4. Check your results

Reveal reference answers
  • (0°,0°) gives (2,0) m.
  • (90°,0°) gives (0,2) m, allowing floating-point rounding.
  • (0°,90°) gives (1,1) m.
  • (45°,-90°) gives (√2,0) m, approximately (1.414,0).

Checkpoint: your calculations should match the displayed values within 0.002 m. If not, check degrees versus radians and remember that joint 2 is relative to link 1.

5. Reach a target

Keep the target at (1,1). Find two different joint configurations that reach it. Export one result as a CSV and record the other in your worksheet. Then set the target to (2,2). Can either configuration reach it?

Check the target challenge

(q1,q2) = (0°,90°) and (90°,-90°) both reach (1,1). The target (2,2) is √8 ≈ 2.828 m from the base, outside the maximum reach of 2 m. No joint angles can reach it in this model.

6. Extend and explain

Download the code and this page, or inspect the formula. Predict the maximum reach if both links are 0.5 m. Explain why target (1,1) would then be unreachable. For a code extension, change the two lengths passed to forward(), update the drawing scale/labels and verify against your own reference cases. The supplied model keeps lengths fixed at 1 m.

Done when: you have four checked predictions, two configurations for (1,1), one exported CSV, an explanation of the unreachable target and two limitations of the model. No grading, submission or course credit is offered.

Model and further study

This original learning exercise uses the geometric projection of two planar links. It is separate from the existing Isaac Sim guides and does not test or replace their environments. Source code and worksheet are supplied above.

For deeper robotics study, see Stanford Robotics Lab's teaching overview and MIT OpenCourseWare's Robotic Manipulation. These are external references, not affiliations or copied lesson content.
